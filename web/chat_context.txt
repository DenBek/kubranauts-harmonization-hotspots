THE PROBLEM
Satellites flag every small square of ground (a pixel) that looks hot enough to hold a fire. Each flag is one "detection" (also called a hotspot). NASA publishes them through FIRMS.
Two cameras made the record: MODIS (on satellites Terra and Aqua, pixels about 1 km, fire data from late 2000 / mid 2002) and VIIRS (on Suomi NPP from 2012 and NOAA-20 from 2018, pixels about 375 m, more sensitive). For the same fires VIIRS reports about 4 to 7 times as many detections. Joined end to end, Argentina's record jumps about 3.7 times in 2012 (45,740 detections in 2011 to 167,274 in 2012) purely because the camera changed. That jump never happened on the ground.
The goal: one consistent record, 2003 to today, in which the camera of origin no longer matters.

THE UNIT
"Harmonized" or "VIIRS-equivalent" = the number of detections one VIIRS satellite would have counted. From February 2012 it is the VIIRS count itself (measured). Before that it is the MODIS Aqua count times a factor (converted). Satellites are never added together (two satellites mean twice the passes).
Only vegetation fires with confidence not "low" are counted (volcanoes, gas flares, industry and offshore sources are removed).

THE METHOD
- Factor = VIIRS count divided by Aqua count for each calendar month, measured on 2012-2019 when both flew. Twelve numbers, Jan to Dec: 6.53, 6.67, 6.18, 5.52, 4.70, 4.13, 4.00, 4.16, 5.00, 5.56, 6.20, 6.59. Lowest in July (4.0), highest in February (6.7). Reason: in the Aug-Sep burning peak fires are big and the pixel-size effect is smallest; in the green season small farm burns are seen by VIIRS but often missed by MODIS.
- Series: 2003-01 to 2012-01 = Aqua x factor (converted). 2012-02 to 2018-03 = Suomi NPP measured. 2018-04 onward = NOAA-20 measured. From August 2026 the data is near-real-time and marked provisional. January 2012 is converted because Suomi NPP covers only 12 of 31 days.
- Worked example: Sept 2010 Aqua counted 7,895; factor 5.004; harmonized about 39,508; likely range 31,233 to 48,664; the usual September (median 2003-2022) is 40,113, so it is "normal".
- Why detections and not fire radiative power (FRP, heat given off) or active cell-days: counts left the least unexplained error (0.095 vs 0.173 for FRP and 0.136 for cell-days) and scale in a straight line (slope 0.99). FRP totals look alike between cameras but convert worse month to month.
- Why one factor per calendar month and not one number: one global number gave a 16.5% typical monthly error on unseen years, per-month factors gave 9.5%. Separate zone factors or day/night factors did not help.
- Why fit on 2012-2019 only: Aqua's overpass time was stable until 2022 and then drifted later (about 13.9 h to 15.8 h by 2026), and 2020-2022 were kept out of the fit so the accuracy figures are a real test.
- Why MODIS is not converted after 2022: orbit drift makes converted values off by -25% to +35% in 2022-2025. It is not needed, since VIIRS has measured every month since February 2012.
- Why the calendar starts in 2003: before July 2002 only Terra flew (morning pass, less certain conversion); a Terra bridge was not built for this version.
- Suomi NPP and NOAA-20 agree within 3-5% per year, so switching between them in 2018 is not a second seam.
- Missing satellite days are treated as missing data, never zero: each month is scaled by days / valid days, and the valid-day share is shown as coverage.

ANOMALIES AND CALENDAR
- "Usual" = the middle value (median) of the same calendar month over 2003-2022 (default), or over measured VIIRS months 2012-2025 (alternative toggle on the Fire calendar page).
- "Compared with usual" = this month divided by usual. Classes: below 0.5 much below; 0.5-0.8 below; 0.8-1.2 normal; 1.2-1.8 above; over 1.8 much above. Ratios above 10x are shown as "more than 10x".
- Rank: 1st = busiest of all complete, non-provisional years for that calendar month. Percentile: share of those years at or below this month. Ranks are out of 23 or 24 years depending on whether 2026 counts for that month.
- Calendar symbols: hatched square = converted from MODIS; solid = measured; "!" = caution; "?" = indicative only; dashed square with "P" = provisional or month in progress (value only, no colour, ratio or rank); "n/a" = no estimate.
- Known events appear as the busiest months on record: Parana Delta April 2008 (15,263 detections, busiest of 24 Aprils); Argentina August and October 2020 (2020 is the busiest complete year, 390,960); North-east January and February 2022 (Corrientes).
- The Parana Delta ratio of 161x is shown as "more than 10x" because a usual April in that small box has only about 95 detections, so the honest headline is "busiest April in 24 years".

HOW ACCURATE
Test: factors fitted on 2012-2019, then used to predict 2020-2022 from MODIS and compared with what VIIRS measured. The method never saw those years.
- Typical (median) monthly error 9.5%; 9 months in 10 within 19%; worst annual error 8.9%; average bias -7.2% (conversion runs low).
- Likely range for a converted month in the whole country: -20.9% to +23.2%. It held in 86% of unseen test months, so we say "about 85% likely". It is not a 95% guarantee.
- A converted month got the same colour class as measured in 89% of months (whole country).
- The bias exists because the VIIRS/Aqua ratio drifts slowly from year to year (about +8-10% in 2015-16 to -4 to -6% in 2020-22); eight fit years cannot pin it down. Values before 2012 may carry a 5-10% level error that the range only partly covers. This is stated, not hidden.
- Small areas: the fewer MODIS detections in an area-month, the wider the range. Aqua detections in the area-month and flag: 5,000 or more = reliable (-12%/+25%); 1,000-4,999 = good (-23%/+38%); 200-999 = caution (-32%/+52%); 50-199 = caution (-48%/+62%); 20-49 = indicative only (-82%/+82%); 5-19 = indicative only (-83%/+169%); fewer than 5 = indicative only (-85%/+626%). Months measured by VIIRS (from Feb 2012) are always reliable at any size. When MODIS saw nothing, the app shows "no estimate", not zero, because VIIRS usually saw some fire (96% of such cases).
- Most converted months in Patagonia are "indicative only" (67 of 109) because MODIS saw fewer than 50 detections there.

DATA SCOPE AND LIMITS
- Argentina only. 287 months shown: 109 converted, 174 measured, 4 provisional.
- Regions are rough latitude-longitude boxes, not provinces: Argentina, North-east, North-west, Centre, Patagonia (the four zones add up to the country), plus a Parana Delta box.
- The VIIRS-equivalent record is checked against VIIRS, not against burned-area maps or field reports. It counts detections, not separate fires or area burned; one large fire can produce thousands of detections. Cloud, satellite drift and data gaps also affect counts.
- Provisional months (August 2026 on) can still change when science-quality data replaces near-real-time data. October 2026 looks very low (about one fifth of a usual October). It was checked: all three satellites had data on all 31 days, and each saw fewer detections per day than any earlier October, so it looks like a real low, but it is provisional.
- A few months with missing satellite days (mostly 2023 and 2024, e.g. NOAA-20 February 2024) may be overstated by up to about 10%. Found after the build and not yet changed.
- Possible unflagged gas flares in the west (Vaca Muerta) were tested: factors change under 3%, so no mask was applied.
- Not done yet: Terra bridge (2000-2002), other countries, province shapes, FRP view, reported events from NASA EONET, NOAA-21.
- What you CAN conclude: a month was far above or below normal in a large area; a season peaked early or late. What you should NOT conclude: that one pre-2012 month in a small area was exactly some number; that 2003-2011 and 2012-2026 values can be compared to the decimal (a few percent is within the method's accuracy); anything about places other than Argentina.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS (answer in your own words, short; do not repeat the question; these are method and trust answers, specific months still need a lookup)
Basics
- Why do we need to harmonize? Joined end to end the raw record jumps about 3.7 times in 2012 only because the camera changed. VIIRS pixels are SMALLER (375 m) than MODIS pixels (1 km) and VIIRS is more sensitive, so VIIRS flags about 4 to 7 times as many detections for the same fires; MODIS is the camera that sees fewer. Harmonizing gives one record where the camera no longer matters.
- Wording care: the likely range is NOT "85% accuracy". Say "the true value fell inside the range in about 85% of test months". Accuracy means the typical error (9.5%).
Method
- Why a simple ratio and not machine learning? The relation between the two cameras is close to a straight scale factor (slope 0.99 for counts) with a stable pattern by calendar month. Twelve numbers are easy to explain and audit and need no guessing outside the fitted range. Where the method fails (small areas, months when MODIS saw nothing, orbit drift) the information is missing from MODIS, so a fancier model would not fix it. A flexible model was not tried.
- Why one factor per calendar month? Fire size and the VIIRS/MODIS ratio change with the season (4.0 in July, 6.7 in February). Monthly factors cut the typical error on unseen years from 16.5% (one global number) to 9.5%.
- Why one factor for the whole country, not per region? Regional factors did not improve the country result (median error 9.3% vs 9.5%) and the regional results were mixed. Regions and drawn areas therefore use the country factor, so their errors are larger.
- Why day and night together? Separate day and night factors lowered the median error a little but raised the bias, and the night factors were unstable (13 to 46). It was a close call and could be revisited with more test years.
- Why not refit on 2012-2022 now that the test is done? The reported errors describe the factors in use. Refitting would use the test years and the accuracy figures would no longer be a real test. A refit is a team decision, not made yet.
- Why Aqua and not Terra for 2003 onward? Aqua is the steadier reference with about twice Terra's detections. Terra alone covers only late 2000 to mid 2002; a direct Terra conversion was tested but is more seasonal and less certain (wider spread), so that period is left out.
- Is the switch from Suomi NPP to NOAA-20 in 2018 a seam? No. They are the same camera design; counts agree within 3-5% per year (ratio 1.025 on matched days), and the factors predict NOAA-20 about as well as Suomi NPP.
- How are missing satellite days handled? As missing data, never zero. The month is scaled by days / valid days and the valid-day share is shown as coverage. A few months with gap days (mostly 2023-2024, e.g. NOAA-20 February 2024) may be overstated by up to about 10%; this is known and not yet fixed.
- What does "harmonized" or "VIIRS-equivalent" mean? The number of detections one VIIRS satellite would have counted. Measured months are the VIIRS count itself; converted months are MODIS Aqua times the monthly factor.
Trust and accuracy
- How accurate is it? On years the method never saw (2020-2022, whole country, monthly): typical error 9.5%, 9 months in 10 within 19%, worst annual error 8.9%, colour class matched in 89% of months. These figures are for the whole country; smaller areas are worse.
- Is it accurate in each region? Less so. Typical monthly error on unseen years: north-east 13.8%, north-west 8.4%, centre 16.1%, Patagonia 21.3%. The country figure should not be quoted for a region.
- Why does it run low by about 7%? The VIIRS/MODIS ratio drifts slowly from year to year (about +8-10% in 2015-16 to -4 to -6% in 2020-22) and eight fit years cannot pin that down. The bias is stated, not hidden; values before 2012 may carry a 5-10% level error that the likely range only partly covers.
- What does "likely range" mean? An interval from tests on unseen years. A nominal 95% interval held in 86% of test months, so we say "about 85% likely", not 95%. It is not a statistical guarantee.
- Can pre-2012 values be checked? Not directly: there is no VIIRS before 2012. The known events (Parana Delta April 2008, Argentina August-October 2020, north-east January-February 2022) rank at or near the top on all four satellites, which is a consistency check, not proof.
- Is it checked against the ground? No. It is checked against VIIRS only, not against burned-area maps or field reports. Detections are not number of fires or area burned.
- Does it work outside Argentina? Unknown. Only Argentina was built and tested; the method may be specific to it.
- Can I use it to say fires are rising or falling? Not yet claimed. No trend test was done, and a few percent differences between 2003-2011 and 2012-2026 are inside the method's accuracy.
- Why does the calendar not convert MODIS after 2022? Terra and Aqua drift in overpass time (Aqua later, Terra earlier), so converted values would be off by about -25% to +35% in 2022-2025. It is not needed: VIIRS has measured every month since February 2012.
Interpretation
- Was 2012 a real drop? Raw counts jumped 3.7 times in 2012 only because the camera changed. Harmonized, 2011 to 2012 is x0.83: a real fall (MODIS alone fell 18%), so 2012 was a quieter year.
- Why does the Parana Delta show "more than 10x"? Its usual April is only about 95 detections, so any large fire gives a huge ratio. The safer headline is "busiest April in 24 years".
- Is October 2026 really a record low? It looks like a real low: all three satellites had data on all 31 days and each saw fewer detections per day than any earlier October. But it is provisional near-real-time data, so it is not compared yet and may change.
- Why are small areas before 2012 unreliable? There are few MODIS detections to scale (under 50 in an area-month means indicative only), so the likely range can be wider than plus or minus 80%. The calendar flags this, and from 2012 values are measured.
- What is the difference between measured, converted and provisional? Measured = VIIRS count. Converted = MODIS Aqua x monthly factor (2003 to January 2012). Provisional = recent near-real-time VIIRS (August 2026 on), value only.
- Which fires are removed? Volcanoes, gas flares, industry, offshore sources and low-confidence detections. Some unflagged gas flares may remain in the west; testing showed the factors change under 3%.
