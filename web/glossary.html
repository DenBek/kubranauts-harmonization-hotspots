Words used

Plain-language meanings for the terms on every page. All figures on this site are for Argentina and come from NASA FIRMS satellite detections. The unit is always the number of fire detections one VIIRS satellite would have counted.

The count

Detection

One satellite pixel flagged as containing a fire, at one overpass. It is not one fire: a big fire lights many pixels, and a satellite passing twice counts twice. We keep only detections FIRMS labels as vegetation fire with nominal or high confidence.

Harmonized (VIIRS-equivalent)

Every month on one scale: the number of detections one VIIRS satellite would have counted. From February 2012 this is simply the VIIRS count. Before that it is the MODIS Aqua count multiplied by a factor. The aim is that a month in 2005 can be compared with a month in 2025. It is not a count of fires or of area burned.

The two cameras

MODIS

The older instrument, flown on NASA’s Terra and Aqua satellites. Its pixels are about 1 km wide. It gives the long record, but it sees fewer small fires, so it reports fewer detections than VIIRS for the same burning. This site converts MODIS Aqua only, and only up to January 2012.

VIIRS

The newer instrument, flown on Suomi NPP (February 2012 to March 2018) and NOAA-20 (April 2018 on). Its pixels are about 375 m wide, so it flags smaller fires and reports roughly 4 to 7 times as many detections as MODIS Aqua in Argentina. It is the reference scale of this site.

The 2012 jump

If you join the MODIS and VIIRS counts end to end, Argentina jumps about 3.7 times between 2011 and 2012. That is the new camera counting, not more fire. After conversion the same step is about 0.83 times (a small real drop: MODIS Aqua alone fell 18% that year).

Factor

The number MODIS Aqua detections are multiplied by to get the VIIRS-equivalent count. There is one for each calendar month, measured from 2012 to 2019 when both cameras flew: 6.53, 6.67, 6.18, 5.52, 4.70, 4.13, 4.00, 4.16, 5.00, 5.56, 6.20, 6.59 for January to December. It differs by month because the cameras disagree by different amounts in the burning season and out of it. Months measured by VIIRS have no factor.

How much to trust a month

Measured or converted

Measured months (February 2012 on) are VIIRS counts. Converted months (January 2003 to January 2012) are MODIS Aqua times the factor. Converted months are hatched in the charts and carry a likely range. January 2012 is converted because Suomi NPP covers only 12 of its 31 days.

Provisional

Near-real-time VIIRS data, available within hours but replaced by final science-quality data some months later. On this site that is August 2026 onwards. The value is shown, but not compared with earlier years, because it can still change.

Month in progress

The current month, which has not finished. It is shown as it stands, not scaled to a full month, and not compared with earlier years.

Coverage

The share of days in a month with valid satellite data. A day with no data is missing, not a day without fire: the month is scaled up by days divided by valid days, and the panel shows the coverage.

Likely range

For a converted month, the range that has about an 85% chance of containing the true VIIRS-equivalent value. For the whole country it is about 21% below to 23% above the converted value. It is wider for smaller areas. Measured months have no range. The "about 85%" comes from a test: the range held in 86% of months from 2020 to 2022, years the method had not seen.

Reliability flag

For converted months, how much to trust the value, set by how many MODIS Aqua detections the area had that month. 5,000 or more: reliable. 1,000 to 4,999: good. 50 to 999: caution (!). Under 50: indicative only (?). If MODIS saw nothing, the month is shown as "no estimate", never as zero. Measured months are reliable at any size.

Tested on unseen years

We fitted the factors on 2012 to 2019, then converted 2020 to 2022 from MODIS and compared the result with what VIIRS measured. For the whole country, half the months were within 9.5% and nine in ten within 19%. This is the test behind the numbers on the accuracy page.

Fit years

2012 to 2019: the years both cameras flew and MODIS Aqua was still crossing at a steady time of day. Later years are left out of the fit because Aqua’s orbit has drifted, and kept for testing.

Comparing with other years

Usual

The middle value of the same calendar month across the baseline years. Default: every harmonized year 2003 to 2022. Alternative: measured VIIRS months 2012 to 2025. Half the years were busier, half quieter.

Compared with usual

This month divided by the usual value for that calendar month. 1.0× is usual; 2.0× is twice as busy. Anything above 10× is shown as "more than 10×": where the usual level is small, the ratio can look huge, and the rank is the clearer read.

Colour classes

Below 0.5× usual: much below. 0.5 to 0.8: below. 0.8 to 1.2: normal. 1.2 to 1.8: above. Over 1.8: much above. Provisional months and months in progress get no colour.

Rank

1st means the busiest of all complete, non-provisional years for that calendar month, for example "1st of 24 Aprils". The number of years varies (23 or 24) because the latest months are provisional.

Percentile

The share of those years that were at or below this month. 100 is the busiest.

Places and sources

Map cell

The draw-an-area page adds up map cells of 1 degree (about 100 km). A cell is included when its centre is inside your shape. A shape smaller than one cell uses the whole cell. The circle on the map sits on the cell’s centre; it is not the position of one fire.

Region

Argentina as a whole, or one of five rough latitude and longitude boxes: North-east, North-west, Centre, Patagonia, and the Paraná Delta. The four zones add up to the country. The boxes are not province or ecosystem borders.

FIRMS

NASA’s Fire Information for Resource Management System, the source of the MODIS and VIIRS active-fire detections used here. Values are real FIRMS detections for Argentina, counted and converted as described above. They measure how many pixels VIIRS would flag, not area burned.
