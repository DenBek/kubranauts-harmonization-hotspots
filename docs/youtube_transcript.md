**[0:20] Why hotspots matter**

I have the privilege of working with a team of scientists who provide early warning and emergency response for events happening around the globe. Those events are fires, floods and oil spills, and they can last from a few days to even weeks.

Almost half of those emergencies are fires: either wildfires in forested areas, or human-related fires from agricultural activities, industrial activities or land clearing. For ongoing fires, these scientists use satellite hotspots to monitor them and to know where the highest probabilities of active fire are.

**[1:12] The burning activity calendar**

But there is another powerful tool that also uses hotspots. It integrates historical and recent hotspot data. This tool is called a burning activity calendar (also a fire activity calendar), and it helps visualize trends, anomalies and seasonal patterns in fire behavior. For example, it shows how many hotspots a particular area has had across different years.

Climate models project that fire weather will become more common and more intense, so both the calendar and hotspots are really important.

**[2:28] The core of the challenge**

Now we have arrived at the core of the challenge. MODIS and VIIRS sensors have tracked active fire hotspots for more than two decades. The problem is that the record is split between these two sensors, whose data cannot be directly compared. Why? Because they have different spatial resolutions, different sensitivities and even different observation schedules.

To make matters worse, MODIS sensors are reaching the end of their operational life and are being replaced by VIIRS sensors. If there is no method to compare their data, almost 10 years **[?: unclear in the audio]** of historical information will be lost, and fire managers will be left without a historical calendar that tells them when and where fires have happened. **[3:51] Two parts**

When we were thinking about the challenge, we thought of it as having two parts:

1. **Harmonization of hotspots from MODIS and VIIRS.** By the end of this phase, it should not matter whether a hotspot came from one sensor or the other. The real focus is on the trends, anomalies and patterns they reveal.
